Step-edge terraces

Step-8x4 / 16x4 / 24x4

Half the top face of a 4-layer (111) slab gets one extra full Au layer, forming a raised strip bounded by two step edges — a fixed-half-coverage series where both terraces widen together as the cell grows. Terrace width is measured perpendicular to the step direction, not as half a raw lattice vector.

Edge classification — open, not resolved. An earlier claim that the two edges were "verified not equivalent" (coordination 12 vs 11) is retracted: that check used the wrong atoms (step feet, not the actual upper-edge atoms) and a neighbor-counting call that can silently miss periodic images in a small cell. Redone properly (check_step_edges.py, explicit lattice-shift enumeration, 3.2/3.4/3.6 Å cutoffs, stable): both upper-edge atoms have CN=7 with identical shell composition (3 below + 4 in-layer + 0 above). That's suggestive of equivalence but doesn't confirm it — registry beyond the first shell hasn't been checked. Labeled "edge 1 / edge 2" in the diagrams below, deliberately not A/B.

True crystallographic vicinal slabs

Au(211), Au(221), Au(332), Au(554)

Cut directly from bulk Au along the stated (hkl) — the (hkl) normal is aligned to z, thickness is set by real geometry (not a layer count that means something different for every facet), and the back termination is left natural rather than forced flat. Convention: A-type = {100} microfacet, B-type = {111} microfacet.
